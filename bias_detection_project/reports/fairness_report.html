How to read this report

Each chart gives every model a fairness score between 0 and 1. A score of 1.0 means the model treats all groups evenly; lower scores mean some groups do worse than others. The thin black lines show the range of plausible values given the size of the test set. When a summary says "clearly", the gap between two models is bigger than that normal wobble. The smaller chart on the right shows accuracy (Kaggle) or, for FairCVdb, how closely the model agrees with the scores it was given. That is not a measure of fairness.

The three models, from simplest to most advanced:

  • Simple: the simple word-counting model.
  • AI model: an AI language model without bias-aware weighting. This is the control.
  • AI model + bias-aware: the same AI language model, with bias-aware weighting added.

The datasets: Kaggle asks the models to predict a job category. FairCVdb has three kinds of scores: one that ignores gender and ethnicity, and two that are deliberately biased by gender or by ethnicity.

For each result, the first box (highlighted in green) shows the effect of the bias-aware weighting on its own. The other boxes show the effect of the AI language model alone and of both changes together.
